# Full Price — Unresolved Editorial Issues

Working notes for future sessions. These are the remaining issues from the initial editorial and publishing review. Denise's expansion, Keisha's campus life, and the stolen-twenty beat are not included because those revisions have already been addressed in the spine.


## 1. Calibrate Estelle's morality around the $900,000

### The concern

The $900,000 reveal is powerful, but it changes the moral scale of Estelle's secrecy. She has substantial assets while Denise works unwanted shifts, manages household scarcity, and sells a watch Estelle knows is irreplaceable. Readers may see this not merely as pride or trauma but as Estelle allowing preventable harm in order to preserve control.

The novel currently says she is understood but not absolved. The draft must make that distinction real. Denise's anger is one part of the solution, but the book also needs to clarify what the money represents to Estelle and why it is not simply a family checking account.

### Questions to settle

- How much of the $900,000 is retirement money, taxable money, stock, or otherwise difficult to spend casually?
- What does Estelle reasonably need for housing, medical care, and possible long-term care?
- Has she helped Denise financially before, or has all assistance been hidden and narrowly directed?
- Does Estelle know Denise is financially strained, even if she does not know about the tuition rescue?
- What relational consequence does Estelle face after the table?
- Does the laptop loan with token interest feel respectful and clarifying, or unnecessarily cold after the wealth reveal?
- How does the story prevent Estelle's competence and historical injury from becoming an excuse for damage she caused?

### Desired outcome

Preserve the shocking $900,000 scale while making Estelle's fear of depletion, dependence, and erasure materially understandable. Allow Denise's accusation to stand without forcing immediate forgiveness or giving Estelle an eloquent apology she would not plausibly make.

## 2. Reduce the sense of thematic over-engineering

### The concern

The spine assigns nearly every supporting character, object, and set piece a specific financial lesson. This produces excellent structural clarity but risks making the finished novel feel like a beautiful instructional machine. Sydney, Emily, Sofi, Lourdes, Max, Gary, and the concessionaire can appear to be demonstrating propositions rather than pursuing unruly lives.

The revised design rule, “Entertain first, then educate,” helps, but the chapter plan will need to create scenes whose pleasures and conflicts are not visibly curricular.

### Questions to settle

- Which existing beats feel most like demonstrations rather than events?
- Which supporting characters need desires unrelated to money or Keisha's education?
- Can any lesson be removed from the ledger without weakening the story?
- Which scenes should exist primarily for attraction, friendship, boredom, food, work, local life, or comedy?
- Are Emily's banking-app beat and Sydney's ticket sale both necessary?
- Can some motifs remain unresolved or imperfect instead of paying off exactly?

### Desired outcome

Retain money as the novel's governing social texture while allowing characters to surprise the design. The reader should absorb the financial lessons retrospectively, not feel them arriving on schedule.

## 3. Rebalance Act 4

### The concern

Act 4 currently contains the investment press, the 80% crash, Essie 1987, Max's public bottom, Keisha's freeze, Denise's watch sale, the paperwork discovery, Gary's visit, the contract reading, Estelle's shame visit, the 529 revelation, and Keisha's confession. This is a large concentration of reversals and disclosures.

The act also remains the least comic section of the book. The bridge-team messages add some texture, but the emotional floor stays high and the pacing model continues to flag the stretch.

### Questions to settle

- Should the paperwork investigation unfold across more than one chapter?
- Are Max's public bottom and Keisha's freeze separate scenes or parts of one sequence?
- Does Denise's sale need to appear exactly where it does?
- Can the Gary material begin earlier without making him feel like a larger villain?
- Where can Act 4 breathe through comedy, an ordinary task, or a temporary release?
- Does the shame visit perform too much of the climax before the kitchen table?

### Desired outcome

Preserve the accelerating detective energy while creating at least one genuine trough and preventing the shame visit from competing with the final table. Comedy should arise from character or ordinary life, not from undercutting the crash or watch loss.

## 4. Reconsider the two pre-climax rehearsals

### The concern

Immediately before the kitchen table, Sydney publicly sells her Premier Center ticket and Emily's quiet banking-app progress is revealed. Both are designed as rehearsals for naming costs and facing numbers. Together they may over-prepare the reader for the climax and visibly underline its lesson.

They also interrupt the direct line from Estelle's demand for secrecy to the family table.

### Questions to settle

- Should Sydney's ticket sale move earlier, perhaps into Act 3?
- Should Emily's account reveal move earlier, become subtler, or disappear?
- Does either beat materially change Keisha's decision to force the table?
- Would removing one strengthen its remaining counterpart?
- How short should the runway be between Estelle's ultimatum and the table?

### Desired outcome

Let the climax feel forced by the Carter women's choices rather than authorized by two supporting-character examples. Preserve whichever supporting beat has the strongest independent emotional value.

## 5. Give Max and Keisha genuine nonfinancial intimacy

### The concern

Max currently serves as a pressure valve, fast-money foil, trading instructor, blame target, and light romantic possibility. The design explains why Keisha tells him the tuition number, but not fully why she likes him. His bad apology and their warm, undefined ending require a memory of genuine pleasure or attention between them.

Without that intimacy, forgiving him can look like the plot preserving a romantic option rather than Keisha making a complicated adult choice.

### Questions to settle

- What does Max notice about Keisha that others miss?
- What does Keisha enjoy about him when money is not being discussed?
- Can the civil-engineering material create a scene in which he listens sincerely rather than performs expertise?
- What boundary does Keisha establish after the crash?
- What exactly is he apologizing for, given that the account and decision were hers?
- Why is forgiveness appropriate even though renewed trust is limited?

### Desired outcome

Add at least one memorable scene of mutual enjoyment or vulnerability unrelated to trading. The ending should feel warm because the relationship contained something real, while the split check and undefined status preserve Keisha's revised valuation of him.

## 6. Deepen Sofi and Lourdes beyond their thematic functions

### The concern

Sofi supplies the agua fresca recipe and teaches the group to price unpaid work. Lourdes supplies counter-wisdom about cash, family obligation, and institutional distrust. Both are strong functions, but the Ortiz family risks existing primarily to provide cultural product and financial instruction for the Carter family's story.

This risk is heightened because a Black protagonist's business is built around a Mexican-American neighbor's recipe. The royalty and retained ownership are helpful, but contractual fairness alone does not create full character agency.

### Questions to settle

- What does Sofi want besides a learner's permit and recognition for the recipe?
- How does she behave when she is not the youngest person who sees the truth first?
- What does Lourdes find funny, irritating, pleasurable, or embarrassing?
- What tension exists between Sofi and Lourdes that is not solely about invoicing family?
- How does the stand affect the Ortiz household socially and practically?
- Does Sofi participate in the business as a creator, worker, owner, or all three, and are those roles clearly distinguished?
- Should a Mexican-American sensitivity reader review this strand once pages exist?

### Desired outcome

Make Sofi and Lourdes capable of surprising Keisha and the reader for reasons unrelated to the lesson ledger. Their relationship to the stand should feel like lived neighborhood interdependence, not cultural extraction corrected by a contract.

## 7. Make the kitchen-table scene less perfectly sequenced

### The concern

The current outline describes a clean three-stage chain reaction: Keisha confesses, Estelle says $900,000, and Denise reveals the watch. This is excellent structural shorthand, but a drafted scene that follows the sequence too neatly may feel theatrical.

Real family disclosures produce interruption, misinterpretation, irrelevant grievances, questions asked too early, and people reacting to different parts of the truth.

### Questions to settle

- Who interrupts whom?
- Which revelation initially fails to land because someone is still processing the previous one?
- Does Denise leave the room deliberately, or is there a less staged way to produce the receipt and portal?
- When does the watch's recovered history emerge?
- Where does humor or mundane kitchen business intrude?
- What remains unresolved when the scene ends?

### Desired outcome

Keep the causal order needed for the revelations while making the emotional experience disorderly. The table should feel like three women losing control of a conversation, not the author triggering three prepared charges.

## 8. Complicate Gary and the “predator ladder”

### The concern

Gary's Tuesday-sized presentation is strong, but the design groups the 1976 fund salesman, the concessionaire, and Gary into a three-rung predator ladder. That pattern may be too neat and may retroactively flatten the earlier men into versions of the same antagonist.

The 1976 load-fund salesman may have sold an expensive product while also giving Estelle access to investing when few people took her seriously. The concessionaire may be opportunistic without being provably responsible for the permit complaint. Gary is the clearest case of isolation being exploited, but even his product and conduct must be represented accurately.

### Questions to settle

- Was the 1976 salesman predatory, merely expensive, or both useful and compromised?
- Is the concessionaire actually connected to the complaint, and does the novel need an answer?
- What exact product is Gary recommending?
- Would the commission and surrender charges appear in documents Keisha could realistically access?
- Is the expiring incentive legal and plausible for that product?
- Should “predator ladder” remain an internal design concept or be discarded entirely?

### Desired outcome

Maintain the recurring experience of women's labor and money being undervalued without turning three different commercial relationships into one repeated moral. Gary should remain dangerous because he is patient, personable, and structurally advantaged—not because the book labels his species.

## 9. Complete the financial, legal, and local fact-check

### The concern

Several plot turns depend on real financial products, college billing, brokerage mechanics, municipal vending rules, food-service requirements, and Sioux Falls events. Small inaccuracies could undermine the book's educational promise and local credibility.

### Areas requiring verification

- SDSU cost of attendance, aid timing, portal presentation, payment plans, and 529 processing.
- Whether a grandparent-owned 529 would appear in the manner the story requires.
- Estelle's investment history, including IRA availability, contribution limits, Deere shares, paper certificates, dividends, and plausible growth to $900,000.
- The meaning of her 2021 shift “to safety” and whether it should be automatic age-based allocation rather than successful market timing.
- The exact retirement or annuity product Gary proposes, including surrender charges, commissions, disclosures, and cancellation rights.
- The kind of security that could plausibly rise sharply and fall 80% during the summer, along with settlement, taxes, and brokerage restrictions.
- Falls Park vending permissions, food licensing, commercial-kitchen rules, sales tax, insurance, and temporary authorizations.
- Whether the named market, Levitt events, JazzFest, and Premier Center calendar fit the story's 2026 dates and locations.
- The car repair type and plausible approximately $800 price.
- The steel-bridge competition history, rules, schedule, and the fictional boundary around SDSU's regional win.

### Desired outcome

Build a fact-check ledger that separates fixed story canon from provisional figures. Change the mechanics where necessary before prose makes an inaccurate detail expensive to remove.

## 10. Resolve adult versus YA positioning

### The concern

Keisha is nineteen, narrates in first-person present tense, spends a summer hustling for college, has a young ensemble and light romance, and learns explicit financial lessons. Those elements can signal YA or new adult. The Estelle and Denise strands, intergenerational secrecy, retirement predation, and book-club climax support an adult classification.

If the balance is unclear, agents, editors, booksellers, and readers may not know where the novel belongs.

### Questions to settle

- How much page weight do Estelle and Denise receive relative to Keisha?
- Does the prose assume an adult reader even when Keisha does not understand the full situation?
- Is the romantic content light enough to remain secondary but mature enough not to read as conventional YA?
- Are the central questions about coming of age, or about intergenerational money and care?
- Should the project be described as upmarket commercial women's fiction with crossover appeal?

### Desired outcome

Draft and pitch it as adult book-club fiction centered on a nineteen-year-old, not as a financial-literacy YA novel. The multigenerational structure and moral complexity must be substantial enough to make that classification obvious on the page.

## 11. Decide the scope of the opening garage sale

### The concern

The garage sale is a natural overture for the book: Estelle's collection, Denise's long argument with her, the 1965 envelope, Sofi's pitcher, the birth of the stand, Max's arrival, pricing comedy, and the watch plant all converge there. Keeping the actual sale mostly off-page may waste one of the book's best social settings.

At the same time, forcing every inciting element into one extended scene could make the opening feel overpacked.

### Questions to settle

- Does the novel open during sorting week or on the morning of the sale?
- Which events occur during preparation and which during the public sale?
- Should the bursar bill arrive during the sale, afterward, or at the end of a separate chapter?
- How much room does Estelle's provenance comedy need before it becomes repetitive?
- Can the sale establish the ensemble without requiring every major character to enter immediately?

### Desired outcome

Give the garage sale enough page space to establish voice, family, objects, neighborhood, and comic register. Spread the inciting material across several short chapters if necessary rather than reducing the event to summary or overloading a single chapter.

## 12. Refine the pitch and comparison language

### The concern

The internal description leads with a novel that “quietly educates and encourages young women to handle money.” That is a useful design goal but a risky public pitch because it makes the book sound instructional before it sounds emotionally irresistible.

The comparison titles communicate voice, money texture, and multigenerational structure, but *Black Cake* may imply a broader family-saga scale than this novel intends. The adult-versus-YA question also affects which comparisons will be most useful.

### Questions to settle

- What is the cleanest one-sentence statement of the family collision?
- Which two recent titles best signal tone, audience, and commercial shelf?
- Is the Sheldon reference useful outside the design documents?
- How should the financial-education benefit appear in a query or proposal without becoming the primary promise?

### Desired outcome

Lead external materials with three women secretly trying to solve the same college bill and the irreversible cost of their silence. Present financial literacy as one of the novel's pleasures and selling points, not as homework the reader is being invited to complete.
