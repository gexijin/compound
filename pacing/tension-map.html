The Tension Map

Two lines, one engine. The red line scores what is happening on the page — the sirens. The blue line scores what the reader is feeling — the pressure of the cards she holds that Keisha doesn't. The Sheldon effect lives in the gap between them: for most of the book, suspense runs above intensity, because the reader knows about Estelle's counting, Denise's portal login, and the 529 long before Keisha does. The lines cross exactly once — at the kitchen table, where every card gets cashed and suspense collapses while intensity peaks.

  • The sawtooth is the Sheldon signature. Every hard out is a local spike; every spike is followed by a valley one to two points lower, never a plateau — that's design rule #1 ("every bang buys a breath") made visible. If two adjacent peaks touch, a breath is missing.
  • The gap is the irony engine. Wherever blue rides above red, the reader is doing the work — the supper table (beat 21) is the extreme case: intensity 2, suspense 10, the quietest scene in the book and the most unbearable. If the gap ever closes to zero mid-book, the reader has run out of cards and the book is coasting.
  • The floor rises. Each act's valleys sit higher than the last act's. If a late valley dips below an early one, momentum is leaking.
  • The crossover is the climax. Blue collapses at beat 34 because confession converts suspense into event. Everything after it is denouement and should stay under the Act 1 ceiling.
  • The comedy lane is the second release valve. Bar height is comic weight per beat — the reframed rule #1 lets a laugh (or a card cashing) buy the breath after a bang, not only a lull. A run of empty bars is a laugh desert: the strip goes flat across beats 26–29, which is why the top of Act 4 reads as relentless.

The lesson-delivery matrix

Design rule #4 says every set piece pays tuition — no drama-only scenes. The cast table names who owns each money lesson; this grid asks where each one actually gets taught. One row per lesson in the design-rule curriculum, one column per beat; a dot means the lesson is delivered there.

  • Read down a column to check a set piece pays its way — an empty column at a hard out or a high-intensity beat is a scene doing drama without teaching.
  • Read across a row to see a lesson's rhythm — the three real physics (blue: wages, ownership, compounding) should recur, not fire once. Red is speed, the counterfeit fourth — it must read as a trap, never a lesson. Violet is the supporting curriculum.
  • The spine lesson — "say the number out loud" — lands late (beats 30/32/34) on purpose: the whole book withholds it until the table.

The money ledger

The plot as cash flow: three parallel rescues converging on one bill. The number Keisha carries in her head is $7,800 — but the real hidden gap is $2,800. The difference is exactly the $5,000 her grandmother's 529 pays every August, the credit she never knew to count.

  • The bill bridge is the canon arithmetic, regression-tested by math/tuition_gap.py: $18,000 real cost down to a $2,800 net gap, closed by a summer's labor with $80 to spare — no triumphant surplus. Estelle's $5,000 529 slice and Denise's SCHOOL envelope sit inside it as line items.
  • The trading detour is provisional — its peak and trough are unpriced until the math/ fact-check pass. Only the $60 float borrow is fixed.

The rest of the toolbox

Beat-sheet timeline (Save the Cat / Story Grid)
Structural beats pinned to page-count percentages. Useful here as a proportion check: Acts 1–3 currently carry ~64% of the beats, which is right for a thriller — the bust and the table should feel fast.
Corkboard / scene cards (Scrivener)
One card per scene, color-coded by strand. The tactile version of the lane strip above — best once you're drafting and reordering.
Chapter-length rhythm bar
A bar chart of word counts per chapter. Sheldon's chapters shorten as tension rises; worth plotting once drafts exist to confirm the outs arrive faster in Acts 4–5.
Story circle / W-plot
Character-arc shapes rather than pacing shapes — less useful for this book than the map above, since Keisha's arc is already carried by the confession structure.
