# Full Price 

## Project overview

*Full Price* is an adult commercial/book-club novel narrated by Keisha Carter, 19, in first-person present tense with no hindsight. In Sioux Falls, Keisha hides a $2,800 college gap while helping run a summer agua fresca stand and uncovering her grandmother Estelle's secret wealth. The book is about money shame, family secrecy, work, ownership, and learning to say the number out loud. The goal is to tell a funny and entertaining story while quietly encouraging young women to learn about money and finance.

## Form and devices

- Keisha narrates in first person, present tense, comic and avoidant.
- Adult family/friendship novel with a light romantic thread; not YA, not romance-led.
- Artifacts appear only for emotional or plot impact. No artifact-per-chapter rhythm.
- Keisha's civil-engineering interest stays specific, comic, and literal.

## Drafting rules

1. **Voice first.** Keisha must be funny, specific, observant, and emotionally evasive on page one.
2. **Scene before theme.** Every scene plays first as ordinary life, then plot, then theme. If it sounds like a lesson, make it a fight, joke, temptation, or secret.
3. **Keep Keisha pressured.** In every chapter she wants something, hides something, and makes a choice that costs her.
4. **Money stays embodied.** Show money through objects, timing, labor, ledgers, envelopes, screenshots, receipts, and dialogue — not explanation.
5. **Protect Estelle's ambiguity.** She is loving, wounded, wrong, brilliant, withholding, and not easily forgiven.
6. **Honor Denise.** She is competent before she is under-informed. She is never the butt of not-knowing. Her arc pays off in one small question.
7. **Keep Max secondary.** He tempts Keisha; he does not rescue her, replace the family spine, or reform for romance.
8. **Respect Sydney's joy.** The book is not anti-spending. It is anti-hiding, anti-denial, and anti-speed-as-salvation.
9. **Humanize Sofi.** She is not just the watched one. Give her ordinary teenage texture and a want beyond symbolism.
10. **Let objects accumulate.** Bedding bag, drafts folder, float, receipts, pay envelope, 529 envelope, and labeled cash envelopes gain meaning quietly.
11. **Let the market witness.** The regulars are specific, kind, recurring pressure — never villains, never generic crowd.
12. **Keep the 529 boring.** The mechanics stay simple and plausible. The emotional meaning matters more than account detail.
13. **Avoid the Act III parade.** Resolutions must arrive through Keisha's immediate problem, not character spotlight solos.
14. **Use cast economy.** No secondary gets bigger unless the scene also advances Keisha's lie, the stand, or the Estelle/Denise thread.
15. **Comedy from character.** The book is funny because the people are funny, not because the plot turns slapstick.
16. **End with cost.** The gap closes, but adulthood begins.
